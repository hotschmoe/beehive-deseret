𐐬Type in Deseret

Write English below and see it spelled the Deseret way. Deseret is phonetic and English spelling is not, so the converter is approximate: a dictionary of common words plus spelling rules. Switch to Raw to type or paste Deseret characters yourself.

𐐀The honeycomb alphabet

All 40 letters, in the order of the 1859 alphabet: six long vowels, six short vowels, the diphthongs and semivowels, then the consonants and the two Deseret News ligatures. Click a cell to inspect it, or copy it.

𐐧Two letters, one cell

In 1859 the Deseret News added Ew and Oi as ligatures. This is a monospace font, so the ligature keeps the grid: the first cell turns blank and the new letter spans both. Toggle the OpenType calt feature to watch it happen.

Each box is plain text in the font; only the feature changes.

𐐒Numbers, in letter-type

The 1850s alphabet had no digits of its own, so these are stylized to match: same height as the letters, same hairline and heavy strokes, and the same ball terminals.

Emblems of Deseret

Private Use Area glyphs for the symbols of the Deseret era. Click one to copy its character, or type it with its code point.

𐐛Checked against the 1869 chart

Left of each pair: the letter as printed in the 1869 Book of Mormon in the Deseret Alphabet (public domain, scanned at the Internet Archive). Right: Beehive. Widths are matched to the printed proportions, and ball terminals are placed where the type has them.

  • Mean overlap with the printed letters is about 0.65 (intersection over union, same height, centred). Several letters sit above 0.75 and a handful of the swashiest ones (Yee, Es, Long A) are still simplified.
  • Not reproduced: letterpress edge irregularity, and the original bold, high-contrast Didone-style modulation. Everything is drawn from scratch; no existing font was copied.
